This is my review of both the book: **The Odyssey** by Homer (Greek poet) and the movie: **The Odyssey** directed by Christopher Nolan (British-American film maker). 

### The Odyssey (Book Review)

I decided to read **The Odyssey** by *Homer* (Greek poet) end-to-end as a personal challenge before the Christopher Nolan movie came out on Fri. 7/17/26. I was thankfully able to finish reading the book on the same day that the movie came out after around one month of intensive focus. Under normal circumstances, I think it would have been extremely difficult for me to muster the motivation and energy to read a book such as The Odyssey, a book that’s been known to be quite challenging to read. I wanted to challenge myself because I had never read The Odyssey before in high school, so I viewed the movie release as a nice, time-boxed opportunity to immerse myself in Odysseus’ 20-year hero’s journey. I have no background in classics, but I've become an amateur classicist and find that I enjoy reading and learning about the ancient world quite a lot. I also didn’t want to pay for a copy of The Odyssey, so I found a translation of the book online for free by Butcher and Lang that was published in the 1800s. It’s written in an archaic style of prose that’s very uncommon today. To say it was difficult to understand the English style is a huge understatement! But somehow I did it and made it through the book end-to-end. Though The Odyssey is a fictional book in the form of an epic poem, it was a really interesting book once you got into it and actually started understanding the plot. I also used a youtube channel **[Moan, Inc.](https://www.youtube.com/@MoAnInc/videos)** after reading each chapter to reinforce the material and that helped a lot. Here’s everything that I’ve learned!

The Odyssey has 24 chapters that have a nonlinear narrative structure. One has to pay very careful attention to find out if the current events described are happening now or have already happened in the past. There’s lot of flashbacks and sometimes even flashbacks of flashbacks as you read the book. I’m going to summarize the book’s 24 chapters as how they appear in the book first and then I’m going to summarize the book in how the plot actually plays out. Only read this section if you don’t want spoilers to the movie!

#### Summary of The Odyssey by Chapter in Chronological Order

<u>**The Telemachy (Books 1-4)**</u>: *Odysseus does not appear. The poem opens on Telemachus, the son he left behind, and on an Ithaca where his household is being ravaged by the wooers.*
* **Book 1 (Ithaca) - The gods in council; Athene rouses Telemachus**: With Poseidon away among the Ethiopians, Pallas Athene wins from Zeus an order for Odysseus’ release from Calypso’s island. She goes down to Ithaca disguised as Mentes, a Taphian captain, and finds the house overrun by wooers feasting on Odysseus’ herds while they court his wife. She tells Telemachus his father lives, and urges him to call an assembly and then go seeking news. He orders his mother from the hall and, for the first time, speaks to the wooers as a man with a claim.
* **Book 2 (Ithaca) - The assembly; Telemachus sails by night**: Telemachus summons the men of Ithaca and lays his grievance before them. Antinous throws it back at Penelope: for three years she has undone by night the shroud she wove by day for Laertes, and a maid betrayed the trick. The assembly gives Telemachus nothing. Athene, in the shape of Mentor, finds him a ship and crew, and he slips away for Pylos after dark without telling his mother.
* **Book 3 (Pylos) - Nester at Pylos**: Old Nestor receives him on the beach amid a sacrifice to Poseidon and tells what he knows: how the Greek fleet quarrelled and scattered after Troy, and how Agamemnon came home to be murdered by Aegisthus and avenged by Orestes — a story held up pointedly for a son to hear. Of Odysseus he knows nothing. He sends Telemachus overland to Sparta by chariot with his own son Peisistratus.
* **Book 4 (Sparta) - Menelaus, Helen, and the Old Man of the Sea** - Menelaus and Helen keep him royally, and both weep over Odysseus at Troy. Menelaus tells of being becalmed off Egypt until he ambushed Proteus, the shape-shifting sea-god, and wrung the truth out of him: Aias drowned, Agamemnon dead, and Odysseus alive but held weeping on Calypso’s island.
  * Back in Ithaca the wooers learn the boy has gone and set an armed ship in the strait to kill him on his return. Penelope, told at last, is in despair until a phantom sent by Athene comforts her in a dream.

<u>**The Wanderings (Books 5-12)**</u>: *Odysseus enters the poem in the twentieth year of his absence, and his past arrives four books later, in his own voice.*
* **Book 5 (Ogygia) - Calypso’s island; the raft and the storm**: Hermes carries the gods’ order to Calypso, who gives Odysseus up with bitter grace after seven years, offering immortality and being refused. He builds a raft and sails seventeen days before Poseidon, homeward from Ethiopia, sees him and smashes it. The sea-nymph Ino gives him her veil; he swims two days and crawls ashore on Scheria, burying himself under a drift of olive leaves to sleep.
* **Book 6 (Scheria) - Nausicaa at the river**: Athene sends the princess Nausicaa to the washing-pools with her maids. Their ball-play wakes a naked, salt-crusted stranger from the bushes; the maids scatter and she alone stands her ground. He supplicates her with great tact, and she gives him clothing, food, and directions — and the crucial advice to bypass her father in the palace and clasp the knees of her mother Arete.
* **Book 7 (Scheria) - The palace of Alcinous**: Hidden in mist by Athene, Odysseus walks through the bronze-walled palace and its famous orchard and falls at Arete’s knees. Alcinous promises him passage home in a Phaeacian ship, no matter where home may be. Pressed about the clothes he is wearing, he tells them of Calypso and the wreck — but not yet his name.
* **Book 8 (Scheria) - Games, and the song that breaks him**: At the games a young Phaeacian taunts the guest, and Odysseus answers with a discus throw beyond every mark. The blind minstrel Demodocus sings of a quarrel between Odysseus and Achilles, then of Ares and Aphrodite caught in Hephaestus’ net, then — at Odysseus’ own request — of the wooden horse. The stranger weeps into his cloak, and Alcinous stops the singing and asks him who he is.
* **Book 9 (Told at Table) - The Cicones, the Lotus-eaters, the Cyclops [*FLASHBACK BEGINS*]**: He gives his name, and takes over the poem for four books. From Troy his ships sack Ismarus and are mauled; a storm drives them to the coast of the Lotus-eaters, whose fruit makes men forget home. Then the cave of the Cyclops Polyphemus, who eats six of the crew, and the escape: the name No-man, the sharpened olive stake, the blinded giant, the men roped under the bellies of his rams. Odysseus shouts his real name from the ship — and Polyphemus prays to his father Poseidon, which is the reason for everything that follows.
* **Book 10 (Told at Table) - Aeolus, the Laestrygonians, Circe**: Aeolus gives him the winds tied in a bag; within sight of Ithaca the crew open it for treasure and blow the fleet back. The giant Laestrygonians spear the men like fish and destroy eleven ships of twelve. On Aeaea, Circe turns the scouting party into swine; Hermes gives Odysseus the herb moly, and she is mastered and turned host. A year passes there before the men remind him of home — and Circe tells him he must first go down to the house of Hades to question the prophet Teiresias.
* **Book 11 (Told at Table) - The Book of the Dead**: At the edge of Ocean he digs a trench, pours the offerings, and the ghosts come crowding to the blood. Elpenor asks for burial. Teiresias foretells the cattle of the Sun, the ruin of the crew, the wooers in his house, and a strange late journey inland with an oar, and a death that will come gently from the sea. His mother Anticleia tells him she died of longing for him, and he cannot embrace her.
  * Then the great women of the past; Agamemnon, still raw about Clytemnestra; Achilles, who would rather be a hired field-hand alive than king of the dead; Aias, who will not speak to him; and the dooms of Tityos, Tantalus and Sisyphus, until fear drives him back to the ship.
* **Book 12 (Told at Table) - Sirens, Scylla, and the cattle of the Sun [*FLASHBACK ENDS*]**: Warned by Circe, he stops the crew’s ears with wax and has himself lashed to the mast to hear the Sirens. He steers past Charybdis and loses six men to Scylla, choosing that over losing all. On Thrinacia the crew, penned by contrary winds and starving, slaughter the cattle of Helios; Zeus answers with a thunderbolt that kills every man but Odysseus. Clinging to the wreckage he drifts nine days to Ogygia — where his story began, and where the telling stops.

<u>**The Homecoming (Books 13-24)**</u>: *Half the poem is spent in Ithaca in disguise. The return is quick; the reckoning is slow.*
* **Book 13 (Ithaca) - Landed asleep; Athene’s disguise**: The Phaeacians row him home asleep and lay him on the beach with his gifts; Poseidon, furious at their kindness, turns the returning ship to stone within sight of harbour. Odysseus wakes in a mist and does not know his own island. Athene, as a young shepherd, teases him through one more lying tale before revealing herself, hiding the treasure in a cave, and shrivelling him into a bald, ragged old beggar. He sets off for the hut of Eumaeus the swineherd.
* **Book 14 (The Steading) - Eumaeus the swineherd**: The swineherd calls off his dogs, feeds the beggar his own supper, and speaks of his lost master with a loyalty that has no audience left to impress. Odysseus tells an elaborate Cretan life-story and swears Odysseus will return within the month; Eumaeus, who has been lied to by too many travellers, refuses to believe it — and gives him his cloak for the night anyway.
* **Book 15 (Sparta → Ithaca) - Telemachus comes home**: Athene sends Telemachus away from Sparta with gifts and a warning. He takes aboard the fugitive seer Theoclymenus, slips past the wooers’ ambush by landing on the far coast, and goes straight to the steading. Meanwhile Eumaeus tells the beggar how he was born a king’s son and stolen away by a Phoenician nurse.
* **Book 16 (The Steading) - Father and son**: Eumaeus is sent to town with the news of Telemachus’ safety, and in the empty hut Athene restores Odysseus to himself. Telemachus takes him for a god until he says plainly who he is, and the two weep like birds robbed of their young. They plan the reckoning: nothing said to Penelope, the arms taken quietly out of the hall. In town the ambush party returns empty-handed and debates murdering Telemachus outright; Amphinomus talks them down, and Penelope comes out to denounce Antinous to his face.
* **Book 17 (The Palace) - The beggar enters his own house**: Telemachus goes home first; Odysseus follows on foot with Eumaeus, abused on the road by the goatherd Melanthius. At the gate the old hound Argos, lying on a dunghill, knows his master after twenty years, wags his tail, and dies. Begging down the line of wooers, Odysseus is hit with a footstool by Antinous. Penelope, hearing of it, sends word that she wants to question the stranger about her husband.
* **Book 18 (The Palace) - The beggars’ fight; Penelope before the wooers**: Irus, the house beggar, tries to drive the newcomer off and is dropped with one blow. Odysseus warns Amphinomus, the least brutal of the wooers, to go home while he can; he does not. Penelope comes down, beautiful with Athene’s help, rebukes the wooers for eating a house instead of courting a woman, and collects rich gifts from all of them. The maid Melantho and then Eurymachus insult the beggar, and a stool is thrown again.
* **Book 19 (The Palace) - The scar**: Father and son carry the weapons out of the hall by firelight. Then Penelope and the beggar talk alone, and he tells her a Cretan tale so full of true detail — the very brooch her husband wore — that she weeps, and he watches her weep without a word. He swears Odysseus is near.
  * She has the old nurse Eurycleia wash his feet. Eurycleia finds the scar left by a boar on Parnassus when he was a boy, drops his foot into the basin, and is gripped by the throat and sworn to silence. Penelope, who has heard none of it, tells her dream of the eagle killing her geese and announces the contest of the bow for the next day.
* **Book 20 (The Palace) - The last night, and the omens**: Odysseus lies awake in the porch, rigid with anger at the maids going out to the wooers, until Athene settles him. At dawn come signs: a thunderclap from a clear sky, a mill-woman’s prayer. The cowherd Philoetius proves loyal, Melanthius insolent again. At the feast Ctesippus throws an ox-hoof at the beggar; the wooers fall into wild, unstoppable laughter, and the seer Theoclymenus sees the walls running with blood and the doorway crowded with ghosts, and walks out of the house.
* **Book 21 (The Palace) - The stringing of the bow**: Penelope fetches the great bow Iphitus gave Odysseus and sets the test: to string it and shoot an arrow through twelve axe-heads. One after another the wooers fail, warming and greasing the horn. Outside, Odysseus shows the scar to Eumaeus and Philoetius and makes them his; the doors are barred and the women shut in. Then the beggar asks to try, turns the bow over like a harper stringing a lyre, and sends the arrow clean through every axe. Zeus thunders, and Telemachus takes up his sword.
* **Book 22 (The Palace) - The slaying of the wooers**: He leaps to the threshold, spills the arrows at his feet, and kills Antinous through the throat mid-drink. Eurymachus offers restitution and dies next; Melanthius, caught fetching armour from the store-room, is strung up and mutilated. Athene’s aegis turns the wooers’ spears aside and they are cut down in the hall to a man — the minstrel Phemius and the herald Medon alone spared. The twelve maids who slept with the wooers are made to carry out the bodies, then hanged in a row in the courtyard, and the hall is purged with fire and brimstone.
* **Book 23 (The Palace) - The test of the bed**: Eurycleia runs upstairs with the news and Penelope refuses to believe her, and then refuses to believe the man himself, sitting silent across the fire from him. She orders the marriage bed carried out of the chamber — and he breaks, because he built it himself around the living trunk of an olive tree and no man alive could have moved it. That is the proof; they weep and hold each other, and Athene holds back the dawn. He tells her the whole of his wanderings in the night, and at first light goes out to the farm to find his father.
* **Book 24 (Hades & The Farm) - Laertes, and peace imposed**: Hermes leads the wooers’ ghosts down to the asphodel, where Agamemnon hears their story and contrasts Penelope’s faith with his own wife’s. In the orchard Odysseus tests the broken old Laertes with one more false tale before naming the trees his father gave him as a child. The wooers’ kinsmen come out armed; Laertes, made young for one throw by Athene, kills Eupeithes — and then Zeus’ thunderbolt falls between the two sides and Athene, in Mentor’s voice, halts the feud and swears both parties to peace.

#### Summary of The Odyssey by Events as They Actually Occured

* **Trojan War (10 years)**:
  * *Books 1, 4, 8* - Odysseus fights in the Trojan War for a decade, trying to retrieve Helen. This sets up why he must journey home.

* **10-Year Journey Home**:
  * *Book 9* - Odysseus sails from Troy and encounters the Cyclops Polyphemus, who traps him and his men. He blinds the monster and escapes, also meeting the Lotus-eaters who make men forget their homes.
  * *Book 10* - He visits Aeolus, who gives him winds in a bag to speed his journey. His crew opens the bag out of curiosity, blowing them back. He then faces the cannibalistic Laestrygonians and reaches Circe's island, where the goddess transforms his men into pigs.
  * *Book 11* - Odysseus visits the Underworld to consult the prophet Tiresias about his fate. He speaks with the dead, including Achilles and his own mother.
  * *Book 12* - He passes the Sirens (whose song lures sailors to death), navigates between Scylla and Charybdis (a monster and whirlpool), and loses men to Helios's sacred cattle. His ship is destroyed in a storm.

* **Calypso’s Island (7 years)**:
  * *Books 5, 12* - Odysseus washes ashore on Calypso's island, where the goddess holds him captive for seven years, offering immortality if he stays.

* **Suitors in Ithaca (Present time)**:
  * *Books 1-4* - While Odysseus is trapped, over a hundred suitors invade his palace, pursuing his wife Penelope and consuming his wealth. His son Telemachus, now grown, searches for news of his father.

* **Leaves Calypso**:
  * *Book 5* - The gods command Calypso to release Odysseus. He builds a raft and sails away, but Poseidon shipwrecks him again.
  * *Books 6-8* - Odysseus washes ashore at Phaeacia and is discovered by Princess Nausicaa. The Phaeacians welcome him warmly and offer to help him reach home.

* **Tells His Story**:
  * *Books 9-12* - At a feast, Odysseus recounts his entire 10-year journey and adventures to the Phaeacians, revealing his true identity.

* **Returns Home**:
  * *Book 13* - The Phaeacians give Odysseus gifts and transport him to Ithaca while he sleeps. He wakes up on his homeland after 20 years away.

* **Revenge & Resolution**:
  * *Books 13-22* - Odysseus disguises himself as a beggar and reunites secretly with his son Telemachus. Together they plan to eliminate the suitors. Odysseus wins a bow-shooting contest and slaughters all the suitors.
  * *Book 23* - Odysseus reveals himself to Penelope, and they are reunited after 20 years of separation.
  * *Book 24* - Odysseus makes peace with the families of the dead suitors, restoring order to Ithaca.
