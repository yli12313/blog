### Background Context

![p2](/blog/20260808_IR_Hannibal/map.png)

Before we get to Hannibal, I have to set the context of who the Carthaginians were and the state of the world Hannibal was born into. Who were the Carthaginians that we hear mentioned in obscure history books or quickly referenced in movies? The Carthaginian Republic (814 BC - 146 BC) was an ancient civilization that occupied the coastal lands of modern-day North Africa, with the heart of the republic located in modern-day Tunisia. The republic started expanding out of the city of Carthage (near modern-day Tunis, Tunisia) and dominated the coastal lands of North Africa and modern-day southern Spain, with dreams of becoming a great power. The Carthaginians were a people known especially for their strong maritime trade/commerce and naval power. The Carthaginians were also a proud people who envisioned having total hegemony over the *Mediterranean Basin*, the region of land and sea surrounding the Mediterranean Sea. But as fate would have it, they had a rival based in modern-day Italy that also had the same idea: the Roman Republic (509 BC - 27 BC; note that these are the dates of the Roman Republic, not the Roman Empire). The Roman Republic derived strength from its strong land army and started expanding out of modern-day Rome, Italy, after the legend of Romulus and Remus established the city of Rome. Both civilizations expanded and they were on a crash course, with everything boiling over as to who would win control over the territory of Sicily. Because of Sicily alone, the two powers fought a bitter 23 year war: the **First Punic War** (264 BC - 241 BC). The First Punic War ended badly for Carthage, where they were expelled from Sicily and had to pay large reparations to Rome; Sicily also became Rome’s first overseas territory. Hannibal’s father, Hamilcar Barca, was the leading commander of the Carthaginian Army in Sicily and this foreshadowed what was to come. 

Hannibal was born in Carthage (247 BC), along with siblings Hasdrubal Barca, Mago Barca, and three sisters, as the oldest son of Hamilcar Barca and his wife. The Barcid family was a wealthy, upper-class, and aristocratic family that had immense military and political influence within Carthaginian society. After the First Punic War and the loss of Sicily, Hamilcar Barca and his forces started focusing on the contested piece of land that Carthage already had a foothold in: Hispania (modern-day Spain). Hamilcar led a push to expand territorially in Hispania to recover lost resources from the war and build a new base of wealth and military strength. He would often take young Hannibal on his campaigns in Hispania, after Hannibal begged to go. Hamilcar made young Hannibal promise and swear an oath at age 9 to “*never be a friend of Rome*.” Hamilcar unfortunately drowned in battle when Hannibal was 18 years old. Hannibal’s brother-in-law Hasdrubal the Fair subsequently took control of the forces in Hispania and Hannibal served as an officer under Hasdrubal the Fair, further developing his military acumen and war experience. When Hasdrubal the Fair was assassinated, Hannibal took control of the army as commander-in-chief at age 26 (221 BC) after years of training under his father and brother-in-law in the art of war. What happened next was nothing short of *legendary*.

### The Second Punic War (218 BC - 201 BC)

#### Crossing the Alps

Hannibal took command of the Iberian forces (Iberia is the Greek name for modern-day Spain) and set off immediately to work. He spent two years consolidating his holdings in Hispania and achieved fantastic results as a first-time general, consolidating all the territory south of the Ebro River under Carthaginian control. Rome saw Hannibal's rise and feared Carthaginian influence in Hispania; therefore Rome made an alliance with the pro-Roman city of **Seguntum**, which was south of the Ebro River. Hannibal, for better or for worse, decided to lay siege on Seguntum for eight months and brought the city to its knees. He lost many troops, resources, and was personally injured in the eight month siege, but was relentless in his objectives. This gives an idea of who Hannibal was as a person at his core: *he relentlessly pursued his objectives*. Rome sent a delegation to Carthage headed by Fabius Maximus demanding answers. When the two sides could not agree on what happened at Seguntum and get to a resolution, the Roman Republic declared war on Carthage and this launched the **Second Punic War**. 

With Seguntum locked-in as his base of operations in late 219 BC, Hannibal spent a significant amount of time in the winter of 219 BC organizing his troops and resources. He had an eclectic set of troops, with infantry made up of Libyan/African, Iberian troops and cavalry made up of Numidian troops (Numidia is modern day Algeria and Tunisia). **Hannibal’s goal was always to attack Rome directly** and he had this goal mapped out in his mind for years. Many in the Roman political and military establishment believed in the beginning that the war was going to be fought through sea battles, in Iberia/Hispania, or through a land invasion of Carthage. Hannibal had other plans. He devised one of the most audacious plans in human history: *crossing over the Alps (the highest and most treacherous mountain range in Europe) directly into Italy to wage war on the Roman heartland*. This was unfathomable to many in Rome  because the Alps were thought to provide the ultimate protection and the idea that they could be crossed seemed *impossible*. But not to Hannibal, who said the iconic quote that expressed total self-confidence and determination at the prospect of crossing the Alps: "*I shall either find a way or make one*!" In spring of 218 BC, Hannibal set off from Seguntum with ~94K infantry, ~12K cavalry, and famously 37 African elephants to cross the Alps and attack Rome directly. A large portion of his troops were garrisoned in Hispania to protect territory, so only around ~50K infantry, ~9K cavalry, and the 37 African elephants actually made the treacherous journey of crossing the Alps. The journey was formidable and extremely dangerous. Hannibal and his troops had to deal with multiple existential threats all at the same time: hostility from local, mountainous Gallic tribes who engaged in hit and run attacks (they needed to be subdued), narrow paths that ascended into mountains that were slippery with ice and snow, hunger and exhaustion, and the rough weather that lead to frost bites and hypothermia. Crossing the alps took two weeks and by the time Hannibal and his troops made it into the Po Valley, only ~25K infantry, ~6K cavalry, and only some of the 37 African elephants survived the journey. It’s one of the biggest mysteries as to which route Hannibal took to cross the Alps, with much debate among historical scholars. But once Hannibal was in the Po Valley, he was able to pick up Gallic insurgents who resented Rome and incorporate them into his army, bolstering his depleted army by the thousands. Having survived such harsh trials and tribulations, Hannibal’s savant-like military genius was about to be unleashed. I think it goes even beyond that: *I think the experience of the Alps hardened Hannibal et al. to the point where they were no longer afraid of anything, and that made them a vicious fighting force that was about to wreak havoc in the Roman heartland*.

#### The Battles of The Second Punic War
Once the Romans learned Hannibal was in the Po Valley with thousands of troops, it was a total SURPRISE to the system. This wasn’t supposed to happen and this was kind of like a glitch in the matrix. The war was believed by many that it would be fought in Hispania and Carthage, but the Romans had to quickly recalibrate and redirect forces to tackle the immediate threat at the door. The way that the Roman Republic worked politically was that two consuls would be elected annually who acted like co-Presidents, and they had absolute authority in times of war. The consul Publius Scipio (father of Scipio Africanus) was recalled back to Italy after landing near Marseille and a planned Carthaginian invasion by the consul Sempronius was called off by the Senate. Forces were directed to northern Italy where the goal was to subdue Hannibal by any means necessary. No one could have predicted what happened next as Hannibal’s military genius became evident to all. He grew from a small threat into an existential threat:
* *Battle of Ticinus (218 BC)*: Hannibal WINS a small cavalry contest where the consul Publius Scipio was wounded severely.
* *Battle of Trebia (218 BC)*: Hannibal WINS a major battle by luring the combined consular armies of Publius Scipio and Sempronius (~40K soldiers) across an icy river and then unleashed an ambush in the form of a flank maneuver, destroying the combined consular forces. He hid ~2000 elite light infantry soldiers in a dry river bed and unleashed them at exactly the right moment for a total envelopment of the Roman rear guard. Sempronius escapes and the Romans are STUNNED by their loss, but are still not panicking yet. 
* *Battle of Lake Trasimene (217 BC)*: Hannibal WINS a battle when he sets an ambush in foggy conditions along the shores of Lake Trasimene. Hannibal knew that the new consul Gaius Flaminius was an over-confident, arrogant person looking for a big win. To provoke him, Hannibal deliberately marched on Rome and pillaged and plundered along the way, provoking Flaminius’ anger. Flaminius chased after Hannibal with just his consular army, not waiting for the other consular army. As his troops marched along the narrow shores of Lake Trasimene on a foggy day, Hannibal’s army, hidden in the hills, suddenly attacked from the front, flanks, and the rear. With Hannibal's army to one side and Lake Trasimene to the other, ~15K Roman soldiers are trapped and they all perish, including the consul Flaminius himself! This loss SHOCKS Rome and they elect Fabius Maxmius as dictator, whose doctrine of the *Fabian Strategy* (avoiding direct battles with Hannibal) shapes the rest of the war.
