### Background Context

![p1](/blog/20260808_IR_Hannibal/map.png)

Before we get to Hannibal, I have to set the context of who the Carthaginians were and the state of the world Hannibal was born into. Who were the Carthaginians that we hear mentioned in obscure history books or quickly referenced in movies? The Carthaginian Republic (814 BC - 146 BC) was an ancient civilization that occupied the coastal lands of modern-day North Africa, with the heart of the republic located in modern-day Tunisia. The republic started expanding out of the city of Carthage (near modern-day Tunis, Tunisia) and dominated the coastal lands of North Africa and modern-day southern Spain, with dreams of becoming a great power. The Carthaginians were a people known especially for their strong maritime trade/commerce and naval power. The Carthaginians were also a proud people who envisioned having total hegemony over the *Mediterranean basin* (i.e. the region of land and sea surrounding the Mediterranean Sea). But as fate would have it, they had a rival based in modern-day Italy that also had the same idea: the Roman Republic (509 BC - 27 BC; note that these are the dates of the Roman Republic, not the Roman Empire). The Roman Republic derived strength from its strong land armies and started expanding out of modern-day Rome, Italy, after the legends Romulus and Remus established the City of Rome. Both civilizations expanded and they were on a crash course, with everything boiling over as to who would win control over the territory of Sicily. Because of Sicily alone, the two powers fought a bitter war: the **First Punic War** (264 BC - 241 BC). The First Punic War ended badly for Carthage, where they were expelled from Sicily and had to pay large reparations; Sicily became Rome’s first overseas territory. Hannibal’s father, Hamilcar Barca, was the leading commander of the Carthaginian Army in Sicily and this foreshadows what was to come. 

Hannibal was born in Carthage (247 BC), along with siblings Hasdrubal, Mago, and three sisters, as the oldest son of Hamilcar Barca and his wife. The Barcid family was a wealthy, upper-class, and aristocratic family that had immense military and political influence within Carthaginian society. After the First Punic War and the loss of Sicily, Hamilcar Barca and his forces started focusing on the contested piece of land that Carthage already had a foothold in: Hispania (modern-day Spain). Hamilcar led a push to expand territorially in Hispania to recover lost resources from the war and to build a new base of wealth and military strength. Hamilcar would often take young Hannibal on his campaigns in Hispania, after Hannibal begged to go. Hamilcar made young Hannibal promise and swear an oath at age 9 to “*never be a friend of Rome*”. Hamilcar unfortunately drowned in battle when Hannibal was 18 years old. Hannibal’s brother-in-law Hasdrubal the Fair took control of the forces in Hispania and Hannibal served as an officer under Hasdrubal, further developing his military acumen and war experience. When Hasdrubal was assassinated, Hannibal took control of the army at age 26 after years of training under his father and brother-in-law in the art of war. What happened next was nothing short of *legendary*.
